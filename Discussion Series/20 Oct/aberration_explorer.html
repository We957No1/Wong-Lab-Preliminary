How the three panels are related

U(sx,sy) ∝ ∫disk exp[i2πW(u,v)/λ₀] exp[−i2π(usx + vsy)] du dv,   I ∝ |U|².

The pupil is a uniform circular aperture. The ideal converging quadratic phase has been cancelled by propagation to the reference focal plane. Differentiating the residual phase gives (ξ,η) = (f/a)∇W, while the Fourier integral gives the full interference pattern. Piston changes global phase; tilt shifts the image; higher-order slopes spread or redistribute the image.

Field curvature is locally defocus with an h² coefficient; distortion is locally tilt with an h³ coefficient. Identifying these aberrations requires comparing multiple field points. Balanced Zernike coma and spherical modes contain compensating lower-order terms; they differ from raw Seidel monomials.

Assumptions, normalization, and numerical resolution

Scalar, paraxial, vacuum image space; coherent point source; uniform clear disk. This is not a vector EUV mask or partially coherent lithography solver. The pupil diameter is 128 samples inside a 512 × 512 FFT array, giving image spacing Δs = 0.125. Interpolated pixels only improve display smoothness. RMS uses the sampled disk; the notebook uses polar quadrature to check exact modal norms. The total FFT energy is phase-invariant, though only the central window is displayed.

The computed peak is the maximum sampled intensity over the whole FFT plane; no focus optimization is performed. Added normalized defocus represents a focus adjustment up to piston: Δz = −4√3 f²d/a² in the notes' sign convention. The standard Zernike normalization is ⟨Z²⟩ = 1, with positive m cosine and negative m sine. Single-index conventions are intentionally avoided.

Higher radial degrees introduce extra radial structure within an angular family. Defocus changes sign at ρ = 1/√2; coma at ρ = √(2/3); secondary astigmatism at ρ = √3/2. Primary spherical has two radial zeros; secondary spherical has three and begins negative at the centre. These colours describe wavefront shape, not an image of a printed contact.

Why only selected radial and angular orders are allowed
(u + iv)p(u − iv)q = ρp+qei(p−q)θ,   n = p + q,   m = p − q.

Ordinary Cartesian polynomials require nonnegative integers p = (n+m)/2 and q = (n−m)/2. Hence |m| ≤ n and n−|m| is even. For M = |m|, a general radial polynomial at that angular order is ρM times a polynomial in ρ². Orthogonalization fixes the coefficients; it does not change the allowed powers.

R2k0(ρ) = Pk(2ρ²−1),   RM+2kM(ρ) = ρMPk(0,M)(2ρ²−1).

The first formula uses Legendre polynomials; the second uses Jacobi polynomials. The PDF derives both and proves general orthogonality with the disk measure ρ dρ dθ.
